Diary — Spring

On Tea Stains and Afternoon Light

A personal log of small domestic moments — steeped in warmth, observation, and the sound of a kettle that knows the day's shape.

By Hana Ji — May 2026
Portrait curation

The diary wants to be tactile. I ripple pages between my fingers and notice the faint imprint of other days. Tea stains are the punctuation marks of domestic history.

Morning Rituals

Mornings begin with ritual: light poured into a room, kettle’s hush, the creak of a chair arranged to face the window. These routines create the rhythm of a life that prefers slowness to announcements.

There is a kind of holiness in small reverence.

Keeping the Embers

To keep embers is to accept a low, persistent heat. It requires attention but not aggression. The diary collects these small embers—notes on language, a clipped recipe, an overheard piece of music.

Letters and Margins

The margins keep the extras: a margin note, a pressed flower, the price of something bought on impulse and later remembered through texture. These extras become the real story.

I write to feel the weight of small things as they accrue. In time they compound into an emotional ledger, one that tracks care more faithfully than any photograph.

Evening: The Slow Unravel

Evenings are for unraveling. Not in ruin, but in soft letting go. The day's narratives are folded into pockets, stored for retrieval at a later season.

Some evenings teach us how to remember being alive simply by being present.
